PROBLEM

From isolated observations to a clearer personal history

Recurrent skin conditions change over time. A photo or measurement taken once may miss the context needed to understand a personal trend.

WHY THIS MATTERS

More than one in ten U.S. children has eczema, making daily care an ongoing burden for many families.1

Source 1: Silverberg JI and Simpson EL, “Associations of childhood eczema severity: a US population-based study,” Dermatitis (2014). The study reports a 12.97% prevalence in the 2007 National Survey of Children’s Health and cites a 10.7% estimate from the 2003 survey. View the paper.

01

Condition changes are longitudinal

Skin appearance and hydration can vary from day to day. Repeated records may reveal patterns that a single snapshot cannot.

02

Context can change a reading

Measurement location, pressure, ambient humidity, temperature, routines, and timing can affect what is observed.

03

Personal baselines differ

A value that is meaningful for one person may not carry the same meaning for another. Phase 2 will test whether individual histories improve interpretation.

DESIGN QUESTION

Can a low-cost system organize multiple signals into a usable trend?

SkinGuard combines portable measurements, environmental context, image analysis, and longitudinal records. The system-level Skin Condition Index organizes these inputs into a structured view of current state and trend.

Important: the image model is one input tool within the SCI framework. It is not the entire SkinGuard algorithm.

What the project is exploring

  • Consistent at-home recording
  • Context-aware interpretation
  • Trend visibility over time
  • Understandable, non-diagnostic self-care support

What it is not claiming

  • Diagnosis or treatment decisions
  • Clinical equivalence
  • Causal relationships
  • Prediction of flares or health outcomes

DESIGN LESSON

A useful system has to work in daily life

At-home monitoring depends on more than an algorithm. People need a clear way to measure, record context, understand uncertainty, and protect their data. That is why SkinGuard treats data quality, user effort, privacy, and repeatability as part of the engineering.
