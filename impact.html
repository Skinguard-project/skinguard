QUESTIONS THAT MATTER

Examples of de-identified user concerns

“What does an SCI number actually mean for me?”

Design implication: clearer trend explanations and uncertainty.

“How often would I need to measure?”

Design implication: reduce burden and define a practical routine.

“Where do my photos go?”

Design implication: visible privacy and data-flow explanations.

“Would weather context make the guidance easier to understand?”

Phase 2 implication: test incremental value, not assume it.

“Would a lower-cost device still be reliable?”

Phase 3 implication: cost cannot be separated from repeatability.

“Is this diagnosis or self-tracking?”

Communication implication: maintain a clear non-diagnostic boundary.

PRODUCT & VENTURE LEARNING

We listened before deciding what to build next

For the Conrad Challenge, we discussed the product with potential users and explored how it might reach families. Those ideas helped us ask better questions, but they do not prove that people will adopt or buy the product.

What the current work supports

  • User conversations and feedback informed design questions.
  • Demonstrations identified communication and workflow concerns.
  • Product and pricing assumptions were explored.

What is not claimed

  • Clinical testing
  • Demonstrated health impact
